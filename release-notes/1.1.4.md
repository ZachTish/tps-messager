# TPS Notifier 1.1.4

TPS Notifier `1.1.4` is a backward-compatible logger reliability patch. It keeps recurring delivery and settings errors visible after a backward wall-clock adjustment while preserving the complete released delivery feature set.

## Changes

- Diagnostic deduplication now recognizes when a retained timestamp is newer than the current wall clock.
- The first matching diagnostic after a backward jump is emitted and becomes the anchor for a fresh 3,000 ms dedupe window.
- The first eligible high-cardinality cleanup after a rollback removes timestamps from the abandoned future epoch instead of rescanning them until wall time catches up.
- A timestamp of zero is treated as a valid retained timestamp rather than bypassing deduplication through a falsy check.
- Monotonic-clock output, the exact 2,999/3,000 ms boundary, high-cardinality cleanup, dedupe keys, console levels and arguments, debug gating, always-on errors, and unload behavior are unchanged.
- ntfy delivery, HTTP classification, retry policy, dry runs, manual composer behavior, settings persistence, commands, protocols, public APIs, and persisted data are unchanged.

## Before/after evidence

- Exact public `1.1.3`, local `main`, and `origin/main` were pinned to commit `bbaf2ad1429b65e5b10d41c43b7ac576d3763e5d` before editing.
- The exact public logger emitted an identical recurring error only once across an initial event, a one-hour backward jump, and 3,000 ms of elapsed time on the regressed clock.
- Version `1.1.4` emits the initial event, the first event after the rollback, suppresses the duplicate at +2,999 ms, and emits again at exactly +3,000 ms.
- A focused timestamp-zero assertion verifies that two identical calls at time zero produce one diagnostic.
- Across 250,000 deterministic monotonic events, both implementations emitted 247,149 calls with the same output hash.
- Across 41 alternating 10,000-event timing rounds, median time was 6.994 ms for `1.1.3` and 7.010 ms for `1.1.4` (+0.23%, measurement noise); candidate p95 was lower at 7.341 ms versus 7.860 ms.
- In a 10,000-key rollback followed by 20 cleanup-eligible intervals, the per-key-only candidate rescanned future-dated history 21 times and visited 200,341 entries. Removing the abandoned epoch reduced that to two scans and 10,303 visits, with identical 10,021 emitted calls.
- The production-style bundle grew from 20,803 to 20,823 bytes (+20 bytes, 0.096%) for the explicit retained-timestamp, clock-order, and stale-epoch checks.

## Validation

- The focused logger suite passed 5/5.
- The full declared suite passed 32/32, including delivery, settings persistence, manual composer, protocol safety, public contracts, and logger behavior.
- TypeScript no-emit validation passed. The versioned build deployed only byte-changed `main.js` and `manifest.json` to the isolated test runtime.
- Obsidian 1.12.7 reloaded in `Obsidian Plugin Test Vault` and exposed both Notifier commands. The palette was dismissed without running either command, opening the composer, changing settings, or sending a notification.
- Source and test-runtime artifacts were byte-identical. Runtime `data.json` remained absent and `.hotreload` remained unchanged.
- Production is not accessed or modified.

## Compatibility and limitations

- Minimum supported Obsidian version remains `1.10.0`.
- No settings or data migration is required.
- The logger continues to use wall-clock time. This patch handles backward movement for a repeated key; it does not replace the existing timestamp source or cleanup contract.
- The legacy `tps-notifier` protocol alias, fallback notification body, fail-closed settings behavior, and Controller-facing APIs remain because they preserve supported compatibility or safety.
- Automatic delivery retries remain intentionally absent because retrying an ntfy POST after an uncertain outcome can duplicate a notification.
- Existing development-only dependency audit findings are unchanged and are not bundled into the plugin runtime.
